Piece 001 · Poem, 1978

Still I Rise

Maya Angelou

"But still, like dust, I'll rise."

Angelou answers centuries of being written down and written off with pure confidence. She lists everything the world did to keep her there, then rises anyway, in rhythm, like the outcome was never in question. No pleading, no permission. It reads like a dare, and she wins.

Where this comes from

This started as research.

In 2025, a youth-led research project with the TDSB asked students how feminism should be taught. It was a small qualitative study: three interviews, chosen by convenience sampling, so it points at something rather than proving it. What it pointed at was consistent. Every participant said feminism belongs in the curriculum, and most of the misunderstanding traced back to never being taught it. A separate open survey running since then puts a number on the rest, and 44% say learning about it is boring →

wearefeminismm is what those findings were built into. The same ideas, told in a way that actually holds your attention. If that 44% comes out lower for the people who spend time here, the site is doing its job. How both were measured →

It's led by Emmanuel Adusi, the student researcher behind the project. The double m is his signature, Emmanuel, stitched into the site's name. This fall he starts Honours Mathematics at the University of Waterloo.
