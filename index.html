Still I Rise

Maya Angelou

Year
1978
Medium
Poem, 43 lines
Estimated reading
2 minutes

"But still, like dust, I'll rise."

Curator's note

Angelou answers centuries of being written down and written off with pure confidence. She lists everything the world did to keep her there, then rises anyway, in rhythm, like the outcome was never in question. No pleading, no permission. It reads like a dare, and she wins.

Where this comes from

What they told me.

In 2025, I ran a youth-led research project with the Toronto District School Board (TDSB). I interviewed students to find out how feminism should be taught and to identify the gaps in their learning. Most participants mentioned that "it barely comes up at home." The students also shared that they thought feminism "just means being nice to girls." Finally, they mentioned that there is little advocacy against the lack of support for female-based sporting activities and female-targeted stereotypes.

In a follow-up survey, 44% of the participants stated that learning about it is boring →

I built this website to address these findings. My aim is to reduce the number of people who find learning about feminism boring to 0%.
