wearefeminismm · The Wall

Five walls, and one that combines three of them.

Each one is clickable and running the same eight posts, so you are comparing feel rather than mockups. Write something in any of them and you get the whole life of a post: it lands marked as waiting to be read, an Approve it button lets you play the moderator and watch it join the wall, and answering any post shows you how a thread reads in that design.

F · the one to look at first

Entrance, then Everything or Conversations

B, A and E built as one place instead of three. The field is the entrance: it opens pulled back, so the first thing anybody meets is how many people are already here, and it names the two ways in. Choose one and the lights fly to where the cards are about to be, so arriving is visibly the same posts rearranging rather than a page load. Write in either room and it is in both; approve it in one and the light stops waiting in the other. It remembers the room you were last in and puts you straight back there, so the entrance is an arrival rather than a toll gate; walking deliberately back to it clears that, and ?entrance forces the door for a demo.

The entrance was rewritten after it read as confusing from the start. It opened on an abstract line and two abstract buttons, so the first thing anybody had to do was choose between two things neither of which had been explained. It now says what the place is in plain words, shows the count with the line that gives it meaning, and offers a single button. Conversations was cut from the door entirely: it is a choice about arrangement, and nobody can make that before they have read a post. It waits in the labelled toggle at the top of the room you land in, found once there is a reason to want it.

Rebuilt again after the first version was hard to navigate. Three peer modes became one entrance plus a two-way toggle, the view stopped being called the wall inside a page called The Wall, and the scroll wheel now means the same thing everywhere: travel through the posts, with zoom on the pinch gesture and on buttons. The thread also moved to the dark ground, since paper-on-light halfway through broke the idea that you never left the room.

A

Pin Wall

Takes the name literally. A dark room, one warm light, paper cards pinned at angles with real shadows. Answers are smaller slips tucked underneath. The house rules are a taped index card on the wall instead of a sidebar.

Strongest at: making it feel like a place somebody built. Watch: the craft has to be exact or it reads as a skeuomorphic gimmick.

B

Night Field

Scale as the argument. Every post is a light in a field you drag through, brightness set by how many people found it helpful. It opens pulled back, so the first thing you see is how many people are there.

Strongest at: the you are not the only one feeling, before a word is read. Watch: it is a canvas, so the plain list next to it is load bearing, not a fallback.

C

Letters

The quietest. One long sheet on a desk, every post set as a letter in a reading face, and every date, count and button pushed out into the margin as an annotation so nothing sits between you and the sentence.

Strongest at: treating what students write as writing. Watch: quiet can read as unfinished if the paper and the margins are not exact.
one at a time

D

Chamber

Immersion by subtraction. No feed and no scroll: one person's words fill the room at a size you cannot skim, and the way onward does not appear for a second and a half. Answers are a step down, not an indent.

Strongest at: people actually reading each other. Watch: a wall you cannot scan is a wall some people will leave.

E

Thread

The wall as one continuous thing. Posts hang off a line that zigzags down, answers hang off the post they answer by a thread that sags under its own weight. Pull back and the whole conversation is one woven shape.

Strongest at: showing that nobody here posted into a void. Watch: spatial layouts get crowded fast once there are two hundred posts.

What none of them change. Nothing is public until a person approves it, there is no downvote anywhere, your own post is visible to you while it waits, one report pulls something off immediately, and the identity warning sits next to the box you type in. The private reflection wall on voice.html is still separate and is never migrated into this.

The Approve it button is prototype scaffolding. On the real wall approving is a person typing a word into a spreadsheet, and no visitor can reach it. It is only here so you can see a post land without waiting on a moderator.
