presentation with the word feminism spelt incorrectly?!" Yes, I did! However,
that was on purpose. Not because I'm changing the word, rather the opposite,
I'm building a community around it, and in the word community, there are two m's.

career aspirations of being a Quantitative Trader.

**\*SLIDE CHANGE\*** → *I do not face what they face.*

I am a 17 year old 1.5 generation Nigerian-Canadian male. I was born in Nigeria,
and I moved to Canada when I was one. Throughout my research last year, I was
able to acknowledge that while I do not face the same subtle sexism experiences
as some girls within the TDSB, I was passionate about conducting research on
feminism because it helps me contribute to the solution.

**\*SLIDE CHANGE\*** → *the research question*

My qualitative research question was simple: "How does the understanding of
feminism affect the experiences of young girls within the TDSB ages 13-19?"

After conducting interviews, and making a video essay, my findings indicated two
main themes:

**\*SLIDE CHANGE\*** → *It was not talked about at home.*

Feminism was not talked about at home, especially in African households, with my
very own father telling me that in his homeland, Nigeria 'They do not believe in
it.'

**(pause)**

And that right there reminded me why I decided to embark on this project, not
for my own personal gain, but to enhance people's knowledge of why they need a
nuanced understanding of feminism. (Salami, 2020, p. 59)

**\*SLIDE CHANGE\*** → *It is misunderstood.* (the word tangles)

My second finding was that there is misinterpretation when it comes to
understanding what feminism is, causing it to be misunderstood. (Brand, 2018, p. 12)
